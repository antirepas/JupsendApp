Why campaigns matter

A campaign is the launch vehicle: who gets mail, which template or workflow runs, and where opens/clicks/replies are attributed. Perfect outreach treats each campaign as an experiment with a clear hypothesis — not a blast.

When to use which type

  • One-time (bulk) — announcement or single touch. Optional Template B for A/B on the first email.
  • Workflow — multi-step sequences (intro → wait → follow-up). Attach a published workflow.
  • Workflow A/B — first send step alternates templates A/B; later steps use mapped templates.

Perfect-outreach launch checklist

  1. Mailbox send-ready; domain warmed if Pro.
  2. Contacts imported with variables that match templates.
  3. Templates written; for workflows, every send step mapped.
  4. Set Lead temperature (campaign rules): e.g. warm ≥2 human opens + ≥1 click; hot = reply or higher thresholds.
  5. Decide open tracking: on for learning; off for ultra-cold if scanner noise worries you (clicks/replies still work).
  6. Preview a few contacts → Send now or schedule.
  7. Watch analytics + Interested; stop the campaign if something’s wrong.

Lead temperature in campaigns

Defined once per campaign and scored only from that campaign’s engagement. Workflows can branch with a Lead temperature node (hot / warm / cold) so warm leads get a different email than cold ones.

Edit rules on the campaign page. Defaults: warm = 2 opens + 1 click; hot = 3 opens + 2 clicks or any reply.

A/B and experiments

When Template B exists, label what you’re testing and write a hypothesis. jupsend can promote a winner by reply rate. Use experiments to improve one variable at a time (subject, opener, CTA) — not five at once.

Common mistakes

  • Attaching a draft workflow — publish first.
  • Missing template on a follow-up step (contacts stall).
  • Huge first send on a fresh domain.
  • Ignoring Interested while the sequence keeps bumping people who already replied (replies stop remaining steps, but you still need human follow-up).
